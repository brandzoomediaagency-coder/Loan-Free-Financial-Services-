Credit Card Debt Settlement

Find a realistic way out of the minimum-due cycle — a repayment plan, EMI conversion or, in genuine hardship, a settlement with your card issuer.

Short answer

Credit card settlement is an agreement in which the card issuer accepts less than the outstanding balance and closes the card account. It is usually considered only when dues are overdue and you face genuine hardship. Before settling, compare a repayment plan or EMI conversion, which can protect your credit report better.

Why card debt grows so quickly

Credit cards charge high interest on any balance you carry forward. When you pay only the minimum due, most of the payment goes towards interest and charges, so the balance falls very slowly — and late fees make it worse if a payment is missed. Many people end up paying for years without the balance coming down.

Who this helps

  • People with balances on one or more cards who can only manage the minimum due.
  • Cardholders whose accounts are already overdue and who are receiving recovery calls.
  • Anyone who has lost income and needs to decide between a repayment plan and a settlement.

Eligibility and limitations

  • Card issuers rarely discuss settlement while an account is current. It usually comes up after payments have been missed.
  • The issuer decides under its own board-approved policy. There is no right to a settlement and no fixed reduction.
  • A settled card is closed and reported as "Settled", which affects new credit for several years.
  • If you can still pay something every month, a repayment plan or EMI conversion is usually the better first step.

Documents to keep ready

  • The last few statements for each card
  • PAN and address proof
  • Proof of hardship, such as a termination letter or medical bills
  • Any emails, SMS, letters or notices from the issuer or its agents

Risks to understand

  • Interest, late fees and charges keep adding up while the account is overdue.
  • A settlement closes the card and is reported as "Settled".
  • Never share your full card number, CVV, OTP or PIN with anyone who calls about your dues.
  • Pay only into the issuer's official channels, against a written letter.
