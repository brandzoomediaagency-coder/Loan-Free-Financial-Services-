How it works

Our Process, Step by Step

What happens after you contact us — from the first conversation to closure documents. You stay informed and in control throughout.

The short version

We start with a free, confidential assessment, review your documents and agree a strategy with you. We then help you put a written settlement or restructuring request to your lender, check any offer before you pay, and guide you on the settlement letter, the NOC and your credit report afterwards. Only your lender can agree terms, so the timeline and outcome vary from case to case.

The six steps

  1. Step 01

    Free case assessment

    We understand your loans, income and what caused the difficulty, and explain the options open to you — including when settlement is not the right choice.

  2. Step 02

    Documents and engagement

    We collect statements, KYC and proof of hardship. If you decide to work with us, ask for the scope of work and fees in writing before anything starts.

  3. Step 03

    Strategy

    Settlement, restructuring or a different route: we agree an approach and an amount you can realistically arrange.

  4. Step 04

    Lender communication

    We help you put your request to the lender in writing and raise any unfair recovery practices with its grievance officer.

  5. Step 05

    Written offer, checked

    If the lender agrees, it issues a settlement letter. We check the amount, deadlines and closure wording with you before you pay.

  6. Step 06

    Payment, NOC and credit report

    You pay the lender directly. We guide you on obtaining the NOC or no-dues letter and checking your credit report afterwards.

Documents to keep ready

  • Latest loan or credit card statements and account details
  • KYC: PAN and address proof
  • Proof of hardship, such as a termination letter, salary slips, medical bills or business accounts
  • Any notices, emails, SMS or WhatsApp messages from the lender or its agents
  • A simple list of your monthly income and essential expenses

Before you start

Stay calm with agents

Be polite, do not argue, and ask for the agent's name, agency and the lender's grievance contact. Note the date and time of every call.

Never sign blank papers

Do not sign documents you have not read, and never share OTPs or passwords with callers.

Plan for the payment

Most settlements need a lump sum or a short instalment plan. Start setting money aside if you can.

How long does it take?

Anywhere from a few weeks to several months. It depends on the lender's internal approvals, the number of accounts, the amount outstanding and how quickly documents and funds are ready. Under the RBI's compromise-settlement framework, a settlement where payment is spread over more than three months is treated by the lender as a restructuring, which can affect what it is willing to offer.

No guarantees: we do not promise any waiver, timeline or credit-score result. Each lender decides according to its own board-approved policy.
