Legal Notice Reply Support

Understand the notice your bank or NBFC has sent, the deadline it sets, and how to respond — with an independent advocate where the matter needs one.

Short answer

Do not ignore a legal notice from a lender. Find out what kind of notice it is, note the deadline, check the amounts and facts, and reply in writing within the time given — or pay, or propose a settlement. Notices under SARFAESI, arbitration clauses or Section 138 have strict timelines, so speak to an advocate quickly.

General guide. The notice itself and the law that applies to your loan decide the exact deadlines.

Why replying matters

A clear, accurate written reply puts your side on record. It can correct wrong amounts or facts, point out unfair charges, explain genuine hardship and open the door to a settlement or restructuring. Ignoring a notice can let proceedings go ahead without your side being heard. Anything you write can be used later, so the reply should be factual and, for court or arbitration matters, prepared with an advocate.

Who this helps

  • Borrowers who have received a demand, recall, SARFAESI, arbitration or cheque-bounce notice.
  • Guarantors who have been sent a notice about someone else's loan.
  • Anyone who disputes the amount claimed or the charges added.

Limitations

  • We are not a law firm. Legal advice, replies that will be relied on in proceedings, and appearances before courts or tribunals need an advocate. Where needed, we coordinate with independent counsel you choose to engage.
  • A reply does not stop proceedings by itself; deadlines in the notice still apply.

Documents to keep ready

  • The notice, with the envelope or email showing when you received it
  • Loan agreement, sanction letter and statement of account
  • Proof of payments made and any earlier correspondence
  • For a cheque bounce: the cheque details and the bank's return memo, if you have it

Risks to understand

  • Missing a deadline can allow the lender to move to the next step, such as taking possession of security or filing a complaint.
  • Admissions made carelessly in a reply can be used against you.
  • Do not ignore an arbitration notice — an award can be made without you.
