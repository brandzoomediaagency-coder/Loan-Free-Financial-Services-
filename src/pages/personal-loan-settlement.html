Personal Loan Settlement

Help preparing a hardship case, putting a settlement request to your bank or NBFC, and checking the paperwork before you pay.

Short answer

Personal loan settlement is an agreement in which your lender accepts less than the full amount due, usually as a lump sum, and closes the loan. It is meant for genuine hardship. The lender decides whether to agree, and the account is usually reported as "Settled", which affects future borrowing.

How personal loan settlement works

When you cannot keep up with EMIs because of a job loss, illness or a fall in income, you can ask your lender to settle the loan. Under the RBI's Framework for Compromise Settlements and Technical Write-offs (8 June 2023), banks and NBFCs must have a board-approved policy for such settlements. That means a settlement is a recognised process, but it is never automatic: the lender looks at how long the account has been overdue, the amount, your circumstances and its own policy.

Our role is to help you understand whether settlement is sensible, prepare a clear written request backed by documents, and check any offer before you commit money to it. We do not lend money and we never receive the settlement amount — you pay the lender directly.

Who this helps

  • Salaried people whose income has stopped or fallen and who cannot keep paying one or more personal loans.
  • Borrowers with several loans and cards whose EMIs now exceed what they can afford.
  • People who can arrange a lump sum, or a short payment plan, from savings, family or the sale of an asset.
  • Anyone receiving recovery calls who wants a documented way to close the account rather than staying in default.

Eligibility and limitations

There is no legal right to a settlement. Lenders usually consider it only when an account is already overdue and the hardship is genuine and documented. A few points to know before you start:

  • If you can still pay with a lower EMI, ask about restructuring first. It usually does less harm to your credit report.
  • Payment timing matters. Under the RBI framework, a settlement paid over more than three months is treated by the lender as a restructuring.
  • Future borrowing. The lender that settles with you must wait at least 12 months (for non-farm loans) before lending to you again; its own policy may set a longer period.
  • No guaranteed waiver. Nobody can promise a percentage. Be wary of anyone who does.

Documents to keep ready

  • Latest loan statement and the sanction letter or loan agreement
  • PAN and address proof
  • Proof of hardship: termination letter, salary slips, medical bills or business records
  • Notices, emails, SMS or WhatsApp messages from the lender or its agents
  • A simple monthly budget showing income and essential expenses

Risks to understand

  • A "Settled" status can make loans and cards harder to get for several years.
  • Interest and charges usually continue to build until an agreement is reached.
  • The lender may still send notices or start legal steps while you negotiate.
  • A waived amount may have tax consequences in some cases; ask a tax adviser if the amount is large.
  • Never pay anyone other than the lender, and never pay without a written settlement letter.
