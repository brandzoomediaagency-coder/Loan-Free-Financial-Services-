RBI Recovery Agent Rules 2027: What Changes for Borrowers

Short answer

From 1 January 2027, new RBI directions govern how banks and NBFCs recover overdue loans. Agents may contact you only between 8 a.m. and 7 p.m., the lender must tell you the agency's details at least a day before a first visit, agents must carry ID and an authorisation letter, recovery calls must be logged and recorded, and lenders must compensate borrowers for losses from non-compliant recovery.

What are the new directions?

On 6 August 2026 the Reserve Bank of India issued amendment directions that bring its instructions on loan recovery and recovery agents into one detailed set of rules for each type of regulated lender. The two most relevant to most borrowers are:

  • Commercial banks — Reserve Bank of India (Commercial Banks – Responsible Business Conduct) Fourth Amendment Directions, 2026, RBI/2026-27/223. These apply to commercial banks other than small finance banks, payments banks, regional rural banks and local area banks, which have their own parallel directions.
  • NBFCs — the corresponding directions for non-banking financial companies, RBI/2026-27/230.

Both take effect on 1 January 2027. They apply to recovery from borrowers in default, including taking possession of security, and some provisions also apply to normal collection from borrowers who are not in default. Paragraph numbers below refer to the bank directions (the NBFC directions use the same lettering under paragraph 100).

1. When agents may contact you

Employees and recovery agents may contact or visit you only between 08:00 and 19:00. Contact outside these hours is allowed only if you have expressly asked for it, and a request to avoid a particular time must be honoured in normal circumstances (para 454Y(4)). Agents must also avoid occasions such as a bereavement, a medical emergency, a calamity or a wedding (para 454Y(6)).

2. Notice before the first visit

Before a case is passed to an agency for an in-person visit, the lender must give you the agency's details at least one day before the first visit (para 454L). If the agency changes, or the lender ends its contract with the agency, it must tell you immediately (paras 454M and 454N). Lenders must also publish an up-to-date list of the recovery agencies they use on their website (para 454K).

3. What a visiting agent must carry

Agents must show an identity card issued by the recovery agency, and carry an authorisation letter and a copy of the lender's notice. Both documents must include the agency's phone number and the lender's grievance redressal officer's details (para 454X). Lenders must use only agents who hold a certificate from the Indian Institute of Banking and Finance (IIBF) after completing its debt recovery agents' training (para 454I); for NBFCs, agents already engaged without the certificate have one year to obtain it.

4. Where agents may meet you, and who they may talk to

  • Agents should ordinarily contact you at a place of your choice. If you have not chosen one, or do not turn up at it twice in a row, they may contact you at home or at work (para 454Y(5)).
  • They may discuss the loan only with you or your guarantor (para 454Y(1)).
  • Any written communication from an agent must be approved by the lender and show the sender's name and contact details (para 454Y(8)).
  • Agents must give a proper receipt for any money collected (para 454Y(9)).

5. Practices that are banned

Paragraph 454Z treats the following, among others, as harsh recovery practices that are not allowed:

  • Threatening or abusive language
  • Posting recordings or personal details of the borrower on social media
  • Sending inappropriate messages by phone or social media
  • Calling or messaging excessively, or outside the permitted hours
  • Threatening or anonymous calls
  • Intimidating or harassing relatives, referees, friends or co-workers, including public humiliation or intrusion into privacy
  • Using or threatening violence against the borrower, their family, property or reputation
  • False or misleading statements about the amount owed or the consequences of not paying

6. Calls must be logged and recorded

Lenders must record the time and number of recovery calls, and keep a recording of their content — including calls you make to the number the lender gives you — for six months, or until any court case about it ends. You should be told that the call is being recorded (para 454P). Lenders must also limit the information they share with agents to what is needed for recovery (para 454O).

7. Locking a financed phone or device

A lender may restrict a mobile phone, tablet or laptop only to recover a loan that financed that same device, and only if the loan agreement clearly allows it (para 454S). Even then:

  • No restriction may start until the loan is 30 days past due and notices have been served; the full set of restrictions, including blocking outgoing calls, only after 60 days past due.
  • Incoming calls, SMS and emergency SOS features must never be blocked, and restrictions must not stop you doing your work.
  • Restrictions must be lifted within one hour of the dues being paid. For wrongful restrictions or late unlocking, the lender must pay compensation of ₹250 per hour, capped at the loan amount (para 454T).
  • The lender and its technology provider must not access your contacts, SMS, call logs, photos or location history (para 454U).

8. Compensation and grievances

Each lender's recovery policy must provide for compensating borrowers and guarantors for losses caused by recovery actions that break the directions (para 454F). Lenders must have a dedicated mechanism for recovery complaints, and every recovery communication must give the name, email, phone number and address of the grievance redressal officer (para 454AA).

What to do if the rules are broken

  1. Record itNote the date, time, number, name and agency, and save messages and recordings.
  2. Complain to the lenderWrite to the grievance redressal officer named in the lender's communication or website.
  3. Escalate to the RBI OmbudsmanIf there is no reply in 30 days, or you are not satisfied, complain at cms.rbi.org.in within 90 days.
  4. Go to the police for threatsFor threats, violence or extortion, go to the police or cybercrime.gov.in at any stage.
