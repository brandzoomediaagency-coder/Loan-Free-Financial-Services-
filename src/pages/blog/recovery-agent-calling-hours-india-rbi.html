Recovery Agent Calling Hours in India — What RBI Rules Say

What time can recovery agents call in India?

Recovery agents in India may contact borrowers only between 8:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m., unless you have asked them to call at another time. This comes from RBI instructions to banks and NBFCs on recovery agents, and the RBI's comprehensive directions taking effect on 1 January 2027 keep the same window. Contact outside it is treated as a harsh recovery practice and can be raised with the lender's grievance redressal officer and, if not resolved within 30 days, with the RBI Ombudsman under the Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026.

If you are receiving calls from bank or NBFC recovery agents at 6 AM, or 9 PM, or in the middle of the night, this article tells you exactly what the law says, what you can do about it, and how to document and report violations effectively.

The exact RBI rule on recovery agent calling hours

RBI instructions on recovery agents set the permitted window. The directions issued on 6 August 2026 for banks (RBI/2026-27/223, para 454Y) and NBFCs (RBI/2026-27/230), which take effect on 1 January 2027, put it this way:

RBI directions, para 454Y(4) (banks) / 100Y(4) (NBFCs): “An employee / recovery agent shall contact / visit the borrower / guarantor only between 08:00 hours and 19:00 hours. Calls / visits earlier or later than the prescribed time period shall be done only when the borrower / guarantor has expressly given a request or authorisation to do so. Further, the borrower’s / guarantor’s request to avoid call / visit at a particular time shall be honoured in normal circumstances.”

This rule applies to:

  • All scheduled commercial banks (public sector, private sector, foreign banks).
  • All Non-Banking Financial Companies (NBFCs) regulated by the RBI.
  • All digital lending apps associated with RBI-regulated entities.
  • All third-party recovery agencies acting on behalf of the above.

What else are recovery agents prohibited from doing?

Beyond calling hours, the RBI Fair Practices Code prohibits recovery agents from:

  • Using abusive, threatening or obscene language.
  • Making anonymous calls — they must identify themselves and the lender.
  • Contacting your family members, friends or employer to embarrass you or pressure repayment.
  • From 1 January 2027, making a first visit before the lender has given you the recovery agency's details — the lender must do so at least one day before the first visit.
  • Misrepresenting themselves as law enforcement or lawyers.
  • Making threatening calls in the middle of the night (clearly outside the 8 AM–7 PM window).
  • Using physical intimidation or violence during home visits — this is additionally a criminal offence.

Digital lending app harassment

Digital lending app recovery is additionally governed by the Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 (RBI/2025-26/36), which replaced the 2022 guidelines. Lenders must tell you the recovery agent's details by email or SMS before the agent contacts you. Together with the general recovery rules, this means lenders, their apps and their agents must not:

  • Access your phone's contact list, call logs or media files — an app that does this may not be operating within the RBI framework.
  • Message your relatives, friends or co-workers to shame or threaten you.
  • Post your personal details on social media or misuse your information.

What to do if a recovery agent calls outside permitted hours

Step 01

Document the violation

Screenshot your call log showing the time and number. Note the date, time, caller name (if given), what was said and whether it was threatening. Save all evidence.

Step 02

File complaint with lender

Write a formal complaint to the lender's designated Grievance Redressal Officer (details on the lender's website). Include your account details, the date, time and nature of the violation.

Step 03

Escalate to RBI Ombudsman

If the lender does not resolve within 30 days, escalate to the RBI Ombudsman via cms.rbi.org.in or call 14448. There is no fee to file a complaint.

Step 04

Police complaint for criminal conduct

If the agent made criminal threats, used abusive language or intimidated you physically, file an FIR at your nearest police station. Provide your call log evidence. Threats and intimidation can be offences under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023, which replaced the IPC on 1 July 2024.

The lender stays responsible: lenders must monitor and control the recovery agencies they engage. From 1 January 2027, each bank and NBFC must also have a policy on compensating borrowers for losses caused by recovery actions that breach the RBI directions. A written complaint creates a formal record.
