Loan Settlement Scams in India: How to Spot and Avoid Them

Quick answer

Loan settlement scams target borrowers who are already under pressure. Common signs are a guaranteed reduction ("pay only 10%"), a large upfront fee before anything happens, a request to stop all contact with your lender, payment to a personal bank account or UPI ID, requests for OTPs, PINs or passwords, fake "RBI-approved" or "court" letters, and extreme urgency. Only your lender can agree to settle your account, and it will do so in writing. Report fraud at cybercrime.gov.in or helpline 1930, and unauthorised lending or deposit activity on the RBI's Sachet portal.

When EMIs pile up and recovery calls start, an advertisement promising to "make your loan disappear" can look like a lifeline. Some operators take money and do nothing; others pose as the bank, a recovery agency or even a government body and collect "settlement" payments that never reach the lender. Losing money at this stage makes a difficult situation worse. This guide explains the common patterns, the warning signs, and the simple checks that protect you.

How loan settlement scams work

Most scams follow one of a few patterns:

  • The fee-and-vanish operator. A "debt relief" or "loan waiver" agency charges a large registration or processing fee, promises a dramatic reduction, then stops responding.
  • The fake lender or fake agent. Someone calls or messages claiming to be from your bank's "settlement department", sends a convincing letter, and asks you to pay a discounted amount into an account that does not belong to the bank.
  • The fake legal threat. A message with a forged "court notice", "arrest warrant" or "RBI order" demands immediate payment to avoid arrest. Unpaid debt is generally a civil matter; real court documents do not arrive as WhatsApp images demanding UPI payment.
  • The data harvester. A "settlement form" asks for net-banking passwords, OTPs, card PINs or remote access to your phone — and empties your account.

8 warning signs of a settlement scam

  1. A guaranteed outcome. No one can guarantee what your lender will accept. Settlement terms are decided by the lender case by case.
  2. A big fee before any work. Be cautious of large upfront fees, especially if the service is not clearly described in writing.
  3. "Stop paying and stop talking to your bank." Advice to cut off all contact with the lender usually increases charges, legal risk and credit damage.
  4. Payment to a personal account or UPI ID. A genuine settlement amount is paid to the lender's own account or official payment link.
  5. Requests for OTPs, PINs, passwords or screen-sharing apps. No genuine lender or adviser needs these.
  6. "RBI-approved" or "government scheme" claims. The RBI does not approve individual settlement agencies or run loan-waiver schemes for individual personal loans.
  7. Threats of arrest or public shaming. These are pressure tactics. Real legal steps follow due process and written notices.
  8. No physical address, no written terms. Only a mobile number, no verifiable office and no written engagement letter.

What genuine help looks like

A genuine adviser explains your options — repayment plan, restructuring or settlement — including the downsides, such as a "Settled" status on your credit report. They tell you clearly what they do and do not do, put their fees in writing, keep you in contact with your lender, and make sure any settlement is agreed by the lender in writing and paid directly to the lender. How to check before you pay anyone

  1. Call your lender yourself on the customer care number from its official website or your loan documents, and ask whether the offer is genuine.
  2. Check the lender is regulated. For NBFCs, search the RBI's list of registered NBFCs on rbi.org.in; for loan apps, check that the app names the regulated lender behind it.
  3. Match the payment account to the lender's name. The beneficiary name should be the bank or NBFC, not a person or an unrelated company.
  4. Get every promise in writing before paying.
  5. Take your time. A genuine offer does not disappear in ten minutes.

If you have been cheated

  • Call 1930 (national cyber-crime helpline) or report at cybercrime.gov.in as soon as possible — quick reporting improves the chance of freezing the money.
  • Inform your bank to block cards or net-banking if you shared any credentials.
  • Report unauthorised lending or deposit-taking on the RBI's Sachet portal.
  • File a police complaint with screenshots, payment receipts and phone numbers.
  • Tell your actual lender in writing that a third party collected money in its name, so your account records the dispute.
