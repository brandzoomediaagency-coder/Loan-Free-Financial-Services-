One-Time Settlement (OTS) of a Loan

How OTS works under the RBI's compromise-settlement framework, what to check before you pay, and how we can support you.

Short answer

A one-time settlement (OTS) is an agreement in which a bank or NBFC accepts a reduced amount, usually as a lump sum, to close an overdue loan. Lenders offer OTS under board-approved policies required by the RBI's 2023 framework. The lender decides, the account is reported as "Settled", and you should pay only after receiving a written settlement letter.

How OTS works

The RBI's Framework for Compromise Settlements and Technical Write-offs (8 June 2023) requires banks and NBFCs to have board-approved policies for compromise settlements. Some banks also announce their own OTS schemes for particular types of overdue accounts. In every case the lender decides whether to accept, how much and by when.

Three rules from the framework are worth knowing:

  • Cooling period: after a compromise settlement, the lender must wait at least 12 months (for non-farm loans) before giving you new credit. Its own policy may set a longer period.
  • Payment over more than three months is treated by the lender as a restructuring.
  • Wilful defaulters and fraud accounts can still be settled, with board approval, but without affecting criminal proceedings against them.
  • If the lender has already gone to court or a tribunal, the settlement must be recorded as a consent decree by that forum.

The framework also requires a settlement to be approved by an authority at least one level above the one that sanctioned the loan, and the officer who sanctioned the loan cannot be part of that approval.

Who OTS may suit

  • Borrowers in genuine hardship — job loss, illness, business failure or a family emergency — with documents to show it.
  • Accounts that are already overdue, often classified as non-performing (NPA) after 90 days.
  • People who can arrange a lump sum, or pay within a short period.

Eligibility and limitations

  • There is no right to OTS. The lender decides under its policy, considering the amount, your history, any security and your circumstances.
  • Secured loans are different: the lender can enforce its security, which affects what it will accept.
  • If you can pay with a longer tenure or lower EMI, restructuring usually protects your credit report better.
  • No one can guarantee a waiver percentage.

The OTS process

  1. Know the full amountAsk the lender for a statement of principal, interest and charges, and work out what you can realistically pay.
  2. Write to the lenderSend a written OTS request to the branch, collections or resolution team, with proof of hardship and the amount you can offer.
  3. Discuss the offerThe lender may accept, make a counter-offer or decline. Keep all communication in writing.
  4. Get the settlement letterBefore paying, get a letter on the lender's letterhead stating the amount, deadline, payment mode and that the account will be closed.
  5. Pay on timePay into the lender's official account by the deadline. Missing it can cancel the offer.
  6. Collect closure papersAsk for a no-dues certificate (NOC) and check your credit report afterwards.

Documents to keep ready

  • Loan account number and latest statement
  • PAN and address proof
  • Proof of hardship: salary slips, termination letter, medical bills, income tax returns or business accounts
  • Recent bank statements
  • Copies of notices and letters from the lender

Risks to understand

  • The account will be reported as "Settled", which affects new credit for several years.
  • Offers are often time-limited; a missed deadline can mean starting again.
  • A verbal promise from an agent is not a settlement. Only a written letter from the lender counts.
  • Never pay into a personal account or hand cash to an agent without an official receipt.
