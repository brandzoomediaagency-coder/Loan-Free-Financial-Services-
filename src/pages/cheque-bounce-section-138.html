Cheque Bounce & Section 138 Support

Understand the time limits in a cheque-bounce matter, your options to pay or settle, and when you need an advocate.

Short answer

Under Section 138 of the Negotiable Instruments Act, 1881, if a cheque you gave towards a debt bounces, the payee must send you a written demand within 30 days of learning of it. You then have 15 days from receiving the notice to pay. If you do not, the payee can file a criminal complaint within the following month.

The time limits

Section 138 time limits
StageTime limit
Presenting the chequeWithin the cheque's validity period
Demand notice from the payeeWithin 30 days of the payee learning that the cheque was returned unpaid
Your time to pay15 days from receiving the notice
Complaint to the magistrateWithin one month after the 15-day period ends (a court can condone delay for sufficient cause)

What it means for you

A Section 138 offence can be punished with imprisonment of up to two years, a fine of up to twice the cheque amount, or both. That is why the 15-day window after the notice matters so much: paying the cheque amount within that time means the offence is not complete. Even after a complaint is filed, the offence can be compounded — settled between the parties with the court's involvement — under section 147 of the Act.

Cheque-bounce matters often arise from post-dated cheques or security cheques given to lenders. Whether a particular cheque was given "for the discharge of a debt or other liability" is a legal question for your advocate.

Who this helps

  • Borrowers who have received a Section 138 demand notice.
  • People whose post-dated or security cheques given to a lender have been presented.
  • Small business owners facing cheque-bounce complaints from lenders or suppliers.

Limitations

  • Section 138 is a criminal proceeding. Advice on defences and appearances in court must come from an advocate. We coordinate with independent counsel you choose to engage.
  • We cannot stop a complaint from being filed once the time limits have passed.

Documents to keep ready

  • The demand notice and proof of when you received it
  • Cheque details and, if available, the bank's return memo
  • The loan agreement or invoice the cheque relates to
  • Proof of any payments made against the debt

Risks to understand

  • Missing the 15-day window means a complaint can be filed against you.
  • If a complaint is filed, the court may in some cases order interim compensation to the complainant.
  • Ignoring court summons can lead to warrants. Always respond through an advocate.
