Loan App Harassment Support

What to do when a loan app threatens you, misuses your contacts or photos, or pressures you to pay — and how to tell a regulated lender from an illegal app.

Short answer

Under the RBI's Digital Lending Directions, 2025, lending apps of regulated lenders must not access your contact list, photos or files, and you must be told the recovery agent's details before they contact you. Record every threat, complain to the lender's grievance officer, escalate to the RBI Ombudsman, and report threats or extortion to the police or cybercrime.gov.in.

First, find out who the lender is

A loan app is often only a platform. The money must come from an RBI-regulated entity — a bank or an NBFC — and your key fact statement and loan agreement must name it. Under the Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025, regulated lenders must report the apps they use to the RBI, which has set up a directory of these apps. If you cannot find any regulated lender behind your app, treat it as a possible illegal lender and report it.

What the Digital Lending Directions require

  • No access to your phone's contents. Apps must not access your files and media, contact list, call logs or telephony functions. One-time access to the camera, microphone or location is allowed only for onboarding or KYC, with your consent.
  • Money flows only between you and the lender. Loans are paid into your bank account, and repayments go directly to the lender's account — not through a third party's pool account.
  • Recovery agent details in advance. Before an agent contacts you about a default, the lender must send you the agent's details by email or SMS.
  • Clear disclosures. You must get a key fact statement showing the all-in cost of the loan.
  • Grievance officer. The lender and its partner must name a nodal grievance redressal officer, shown on the app and website.

Regulated lender or illegal app?

Who this helps

  • Borrowers being threatened, shamed or flooded with calls by a loan app's collection team.
  • People whose contacts have received messages, or whose photos have been misused or morphed.
  • Anyone with several small app loans who needs to know which are legitimate and what is really owed.

Limitations

  • A complaint does not cancel money genuinely owed to a regulated lender, which can still recover lawfully.
  • We cannot remove content from other people's phones or guarantee that an illegal operator will stop. Police and cybercrime authorities have those powers.
  • If you are being extorted or threatened, go to the police straight away.

Evidence to keep

  • App name, the lender named in the app, and your loan details
  • Screenshots of threats and of messages sent to your contacts
  • Call logs and the numbers used
  • Proof of every payment you made, and where it was paid
  • The permissions the app asked for

Stay safe

  • Do not take a new app loan to repay an old one — this is how many people end up trapped.
  • Never pay into a personal UPI ID or share OTPs.
  • Tell close contacts early that they may receive messages, and not to pay anyone.
  • Review app permissions on your phone, and uninstall apps only after you have saved the evidence.

First to the lender's nodal grievance redressal officer. If there is no reply in 30 days, or you are unhappy with it, complain to the RBI Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in. Unauthorised lenders can be reported on RBI's Sachet portal.

Money borrowed from a regulated lender still has to be repaid, even if its agents behaved badly — the two issues are dealt with separately. Where the "lender" is unregulated, get advice before paying anything more.

Regulated lenders must offer a cooling-off period, set by their board, during which you can exit the loan by repaying the principal and the proportionate cost without a penalty. Check your key fact statement for the period.
