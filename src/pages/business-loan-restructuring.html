Business & MSME Loan Restructuring

Help preparing a restructuring or settlement request when working-capital or term-loan repayments no longer match your cash flow.

Short answer

Restructuring changes the terms of a business loan — for example a longer tenure, lower instalments or a revised schedule — so a viable business can keep paying. Settlement closes the loan for a reduced amount. The lender decides either way, based on its policy and your business's cash flow, so a clear plan backed by accounts matters.

When restructuring makes sense

If sales have fallen, a major customer is paying late or costs have risen, a business can struggle with EMIs and working-capital limits even though it is still viable. Approaching the lender early — before accounts become non-performing — usually gives you more options. Lenders want to see that the business can recover and repay on revised terms, so the request should include realistic projections, not just a statement of difficulty.

Where the business is no longer viable, or the owner can arrange funds to close the loan, a one-time settlement may be discussed instead.

Who this helps

  • Small business owners and traders with term loans, working-capital limits or equipment loans under pressure.
  • Proprietors whose personal and business finances are linked through guarantees or collateral.
  • Businesses that have received demand notices, or whose accounts are close to becoming non-performing.

Eligibility and limitations

  • Restructuring is at the lender's discretion and depends on its policy and your business's viability.
  • A restructured account may be reported as such and can affect future borrowing.
  • Personal guarantees and collateral remain relevant. A settlement with the borrower does not automatically release a guarantor unless the letter says so.
  • Secured loans can be enforced through the SARFAESI Act, 2002, which has its own notices and timelines. Read legal notice support.

Documents to keep ready

  • Sanction letters, loan agreements and latest statements for every facility
  • Business registration, GST registration and KYC of the owners
  • Recent bank statements, GST returns, and the last two years' accounts or income tax returns
  • A list of receivables and payables, and any orders in hand
  • Any notices from the lender

Restructuring and settlement for a business

Restructuring compared with settlement for a business loan
RestructuringSettlement
The loanContinues on new termsClosed on payment of the agreed amount
What you payThe full amount, over a revised scheduleA reduced amount, usually as a lump sum
Banking relationshipUsually continuesUsually ends; fresh credit from that lender only after a cooling period of at least 12 months
Best whenThe business is viable but needs timeThe business cannot recover, or funds are available to close the loan

Risks to understand

  • A longer tenure usually means paying more interest overall.
  • Missing payments on restructured terms can lead quickly to recovery action.
  • Post-dated cheques given to a lender can lead to Section 138 proceedings if they bounce.
  • No adviser can guarantee that a lender will restructure or settle.
