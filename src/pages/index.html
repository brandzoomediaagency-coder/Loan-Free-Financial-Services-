Frequently Asked Questions

Straight answers to the questions we hear most.

Yes. A settlement (often called a one-time settlement, or OTS) is a negotiated agreement in which a lender accepts less than the full amount due to close an account. The RBI's Framework for Compromise Settlements (8 June 2023) requires lenders to have board-approved policies for such settlements. Whether to accept is always the lender's decision.

RBI rules say agents should discuss your loan only with you (or a guarantor), and they prohibit intimidating or harassing your relatives, friends or co-workers. Keep a record of any such calls and raise them in writing with the lender's grievance officer. If the lender does not resolve it, you can complain under the Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026.

Between 8:00 a.m. and 7:00 p.m., unless you have asked them to call at another time. Excessive calls, or calls outside these hours, are treated as harsh recovery practices. Note the date, time and number of each call so you can raise it with the lender.

Agents may visit, but must be civil, carry identification and stay within 8:00 a.m.–7:00 p.m. Under RBI directions that take effect on 1 January 2027, banks and NBFCs must tell you the recovery agency's details at least one day before its first visit, and agents must carry an authorisation letter and a copy of that notice. Threats, force or public humiliation are prohibited.

Yes. An account closed for less than the full amount is usually reported as "Settled", which lenders view less favourably than "Closed" and which can lower your score. It is generally better than an account that stays in default. Rebuilding takes time and consistent on-time payments; we do not promise any score outcome.
