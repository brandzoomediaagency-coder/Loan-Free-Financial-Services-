Loan Settlement Scenario Calculator

Explore how a hypothetical settlement amount fits your monthly budget and understand the savings effort involved.

What this calculator does

It helps you look at one question calmly: if you were to explore a settlement, could your monthly budget realistically build the amount, and how long might that take? You enter your own figures, including any hypothetical amount you want to test. The calculator then shows how much your budget leaves each month and how long saving the remaining amount would take at that pace.

It does not suggest a settlement amount or percentage. There is no standard figure that banks or NBFCs accept.

How the calculations work

The calculator assumes your income and expenses stay the same each month. Real budgets change, and interest or charges on the loan may keep adding up while you save.

What it cannot predict

  • Whether your lender will agree to settle at all — settlement is never a right.
  • The amount a lender may accept, or whether it will accept payment in instalments.
  • How long a lender takes to decide, or how your account balance will change while you save.
  • The effect on your credit report beyond the general point that a settled account is reported as "Settled".

What affects a lender's actual decision

Under the RBI's Framework for Compromise Settlements and Technical Write-offs (8 June 2023), banks and NBFCs settle accounts under their own board-approved policies. In practice a lender will usually look at:

  • its own policy and approval process;
  • how long the account has been overdue and the amount involved;
  • any security or guarantee linked to the loan;
  • your documented circumstances, such as a job loss or medical costs;
  • whether you can pay a lump sum, and by when.

Under the same framework, a settlement paid over more than three months is treated by the lender as a restructuring, and the lender that settles with you must wait at least 12 months (for non-farm loans) before lending to you again.

Risks of loan settlement

  • A "Settled" status can make loans and credit cards harder to get for several years.
  • Interest and charges usually keep building until an agreement is reached.
  • The lender may continue recovery or legal steps while you save or negotiate.
  • Missing EMIs on purpose to "qualify" for settlement adds charges and harms your credit report.
  • Pay only into the lender's official account, and only against a written settlement letter.

Other options to explore

  • Restructuring: a lower EMI or longer tenure if you can still pay something each month. See settlement vs restructuring.
  • Talking to your lender early: before an account falls far behind, lenders may have more options.
  • Debt consolidation: only if your credit is still good enough for a cheaper loan. See consolidation vs settlement.
  • Getting help with several loans: list every loan and decide what to tackle first. See what to do if you can't pay your EMI.
