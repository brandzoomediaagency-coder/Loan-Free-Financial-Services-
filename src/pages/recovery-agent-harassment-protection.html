Recovery Agent Harassment Support

Know what recovery agents are allowed to do, record what is happening, and raise breaches with the lender, the RBI Ombudsman or the police.

Short answer

Lenders may recover overdue loans, but RBI rules forbid threats, abuse, public shaming and harassing your family, friends or colleagues. From 1 January 2027, agents of banks and NBFCs may contact you only between 8 a.m. and 7 p.m. and must carry ID and an authorisation letter. Record breaches, complain in writing to the lender, then escalate to the RBI Ombudsman.

The rules recovery agents must follow

The lender is responsible for the agents it uses. On 6 August 2026 the RBI issued detailed directions on recovery for banks (RBI/2026-27/223) and NBFCs (RBI/2026-27/230), which take effect on 1 January 2027. Among other things, they require that:

  • Agents contact or visit you only between 08:00 and 19:00, unless you have asked for a different time, and respect a request to avoid a particular time.
  • The lender tells you the recovery agency's details at least one day before its first visit, and tells you immediately if the agency changes.
  • Agents show an identity card and carry an authorisation letter and a copy of that notice, which must include the phone number of the lender's grievance redressal officer.
  • Agents discuss the loan only with you or your guarantor, behave civilly, and avoid occasions such as a bereavement, medical emergency or wedding.
  • The lender logs the time and number of recovery calls and keeps recordings for six months.
  • The lender's recovery policy includes compensation for losses caused by recovery actions that break the directions.

What agents must never do

The directions list practices that are not allowed, including:

  • Using threatening or abusive language
  • Posting your recordings or personal details on social media
  • Calling or messaging excessively, or outside the permitted hours
  • Intimidating or harassing your relatives, referees, friends or co-workers, or humiliating you publicly
  • Using or threatening violence against you, your family, your property or your reputation
  • Making false or misleading statements about how much you owe or what will happen if you do not pay

Who this helps

  • Borrowers receiving repeated, abusive or late-night recovery calls.
  • People whose family, employer or neighbours are being contacted.
  • Anyone visited by agents who will not show identification or authorisation.

Limitations

  • A complaint about harassment does not cancel the debt. Lenders can still recover lawfully, including through courts or arbitration.
  • We cannot promise that calls will stop. We help you use the rules properly and escalate where they are broken.
  • If you are threatened with violence, go to the police immediately — do not wait for a lender's reply.

Evidence to keep

  • A log of every call and visit: date, time, number, name and what was said
  • Screenshots of messages, and call recordings where you have them
  • Names of anyone else who was contacted, and what they were told
  • Copies of any notice, ID or authorisation letter shown to you
  • Copies of your complaints and the lender's replies
