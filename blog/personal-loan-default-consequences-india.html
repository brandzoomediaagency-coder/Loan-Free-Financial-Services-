Personal Loan Default: Legal Consequences & Your Options

March 20, 2024 | Debt Resolution Lawyers

Personal loans are unsecured, meaning you don't provide collateral. While this means the bank can't seize your house immediately, it doesn't mean there are no consequences for defaulting. Understanding what follows a default can help you prepare and take the right legal steps.

The Timeline of a Default

  • 1-30 Days: Reminder calls and late fee notifications.
  • 31-90 Days: Account is marked as "Minor Default". Persistent recovery calls begin.
  • After 90 Days: The account becomes an NPA (Non-Performing Asset). Intense recovery and legal notices start.

Legal Actions Banks Can Take

  1. Legal Notice: A formal notice from the bank's lawyer demanding full payment.
  2. Civil Suit: The bank may file a summary suit for recovery of dues.
  3. Arbitration: Many loan agreements have an arbitration clause for faster dispute resolution.

Your Legal Options

A default is not the end of the world. You can negotiate for an EMI restructuring, a moratorium, or eventually, a settlement. The key is to communicate proactively through legal channels. Ignoring the bank is usually the worst strategy; engaging with them with a lawyer's help usually leads to a much better outcome.
