Unable to Pay Your Personal Loan EMI? Here's What to Do in India

Published: 18 August 2026  |  By Loan Free Financial Services Advisory Team

Quick answer — what to do if you cannot pay EMI

Act immediately and proactively — do not go silent. Contact your lender before or at the earliest opportunity after the missed EMI. Explain your hardship and request formal relief options: restructuring, moratorium or in severe cases, settlement. Proactive borrowers consistently get better outcomes than those who avoid the bank. This guide walks through every option clearly.

Facing an EMI you cannot pay is one of the most stressful financial situations an Indian borrower can find themselves in. Whether the cause is a job loss, a medical emergency, a business failure or a sudden drop in income — the emotional and practical pressure is real. What matters most right now is that you act clearly, know your rights, and understand exactly what options are available to you.

Step 1 — Contact your lender immediately

The single most important thing you can do is not go silent. Banks and NBFCs are far more willing to work with borrowers who communicate proactively than those who simply stop paying without explanation.

  • Call or email your bank's Branch Manager or Relationship Manager — not just the generic customer care number.
  • Explain your situation clearly and request a formal meeting or written communication.
  • Ask specifically about available options: restructuring, moratorium or OTS.
  • Keep a record of every communication — email is better than phone for creating a paper trail.

Step 2 — Document your hardship

Lenders make decisions based on documented evidence of genuine hardship. Gather:

  • Job loss / salary reduction: Termination letter, resignation acceptance, salary slips showing reduction.
  • Medical emergency: Hospital bills, discharge summary, doctor's certificate.
  • Business failure: GST returns showing declined revenue, bank statements, closure documents if applicable.
  • Natural calamity: Official government certification where applicable.

Step 3 — Understand your options

Depending on your situation, one of the following options may apply:

Loan Restructuring

Extended repayment tenure → lower monthly EMI. The loan stays open; the outstanding does not reduce. Best for temporary hardship when income is expected to recover. Less CIBIL impact than settlement.

Settlement vs Restructuring

EMI Moratorium

Temporary pause in EMI payments (typically 1–3 months) at the lender's discretion. Interest continues to accrue during the pause — your total outstanding increases. Best for very short-term income disruption only.

Loan Settlement (OTS)

Close the loan for a negotiated lump sum less than total outstanding. Marks CIBIL as "Settled." This is a last resort for severe, long-term hardship where you cannot realistically repay in full. Do not use this unless necessary.

OTS Guide

Refinancing

Transfer the loan to another lender at a lower interest rate — reduces future EMI burden. Only viable if your credit profile is still reasonably good (generally, before going NPA).

What happens if you don't pay EMI — the timeline

Day 1–30 (Overdue)

Late payment fee charged (typically 1–3% of overdue amount). Reminder calls/messages from lender.

Day 31–60 (SMA-1)

Account classified as "Special Mention Account 1" internally. More active recovery calls. CIBIL may be notified. Lender may assign a recovery team.

Day 61–90 (SMA-2)

Account at "Special Mention Account 2" status. Recovery pressure intensifies. Lender may send formal demand notice.

Day 90+ (NPA)

Non-Performing Asset. Significant CIBIL damage. Formal legal recovery may begin — civil suit, arbitration, or recovery agency. OTS may become available at this stage.

Your rights as a borrower during EMI default

Even if you have missed EMIs, you have rights. Under the RBI Fair Practices Code:

  • Recovery agents can only contact you between 8:00 AM and 7:00 PM.
  • Agents cannot use abusive or threatening language.
  • Agents cannot contact your family or employer to embarrass you.
  • The lender must notify you of the recovery agency assigned to your account.
  • You have the right to file a complaint with the lender's Grievance Redressal Officer and escalate to the RBI Ombudsman at cms.rbi.org.in.
Important: Settlement (OTS) is a serious step that permanently affects your CIBIL report. Always explore restructuring and moratorium options first. Only consider OTS if you genuinely cannot repay the full outstanding in any realistic timeframe. Seek professional guidance before making this decision.
